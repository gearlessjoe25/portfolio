Tap In: A music promotion
and discovery platform

Music is growing in unique ways, yet emerging talent often struggles to find dedicated room to flourish. "Tap In" is a music promotion & discussion platform designed to help artists grow their presence while empowering listeners to discover fresh sounds. As the visionary behind the platform, I blended retro OS aesthetics with modern design systems. This was a semester project for Interaction Design II, where I led a team of three to build an interactive prototype using Lean UX methodology, leveraging FigJam and Figma for research, user flows, and interface design.

Interaction Design II • Jan 2026 - Apr 2026
•

The Problem:

The modern music industry lacks a dedicated, unified space for organic grassroots discovery and authentic community engagement.

  • Growth is increasingly difficult for independent artists in an oversaturated streaming landscape.
  • Music discovery feels fractured across algorithms that prioritize viral hits over personal resonance.
  • Artists and fans are forced onto fragmented general-purpose social platforms not tailored to music creators.
  • Aspiring creatives struggle to find the collaborative networking channels needed to produce new work.

Solutions:

Addressing the core needs of artists and listeners.

🌟

Spotlight

Provides artists with equitable exposure opportunities, while helping listeners discover new artists, tracks, and genres—delivering mutual value to creators and fans alike.

🔀

Randomization

Deepens discoverability through randomized track suggestions, turning sonic exploration into an engaging, gamified journey while elevating under-the-radar songs.

🤝

Connecting

Facilitates direct creative networking for collaborations, studio sessions, and feedback through streamlined messaging tools.

💬

Discussions

Gives music enthusiasts a purpose-built hub to share reviews, track breakdowns, and playlists with contextual listening tools.

🌍

Community

Empowers fans to form genre-based sub-communities and fan spaces, filling the void left by fragmented mainstream social groups.

Lessons Learned:

  • As team lead, I learned what genuine project management requires: clear delegation, open communication, and aligning responsibilities with each teammate's core strengths.
  • Working within fixed semester deadlines necessitated smart MVP scoping. Learning to scale back features gracefully taught me how to protect product quality without sacrificing the core vision.
  • Conducting user interviews and usability tests reinforced the value of observational research. Asking open-ended prompts rather than leading questions unlocked our most valuable usability breakthroughs.
  • Pushed myself to incorporate rich micro-interactions and animations, reinforcing my mastery of Figma components, variants, and interactive states.
  • Embraced iterative rest. Just as in music production where taking a step back improves mixing decisions, taking breaks prevents creative burnout and leads to far sharper design outputs.

Mixr: A new way to discover music

Finding new music can be a drag in this oversaturated environment. "Mixr" tackles that drag and helps users discover new music, with a platform built for discussion. This was a group project for our Senior Capstone Showcase to create a working prototype, following the Goal-Directed Design method to conduct research, user interviews, and usability tests. This project spanned from January 2026 to April 2026, using Figjam, Google Forms, Google Docs, and Figma for research and building.

Capstone Showcase • January 2026 - April 2026

The Problem:

Certain suggestion methods aren't cutting it, and it's difficult to find something that speaks to you.

  • On main stream platforms, suggestions are hit or miss, usually dishing users what is popular, instead of what is tailored to the listener.
  • Discussion is often limited, if it exists at all on a music platform.
  • Many platforms usually have features locked behind a paywall, limiting the typical user immensely.
  • Many platforms expect users to understand how to use them, often having poor learnability.

Solutions:

How we approached these issues.

🧠

Tailored feeds

Feed is based off the users interests from what they selected when making an account, and from their interactions on the platform.

💬

Expanded Discussion

Fan pages dedicated to discussions based on music, beyond the platforms review capabilities.

🔓

Open Platform

Nothing was made on this platform with the intentions of locking it behind a paywall. Everyone can enjoy and take part in all features.

✨

Simplistic UI

Simple interface for learnability, with items clearly labeled to ease new users onto the platform.

Lessons Learned:

  • Spring 2026 was one of the most demanding semesters of my academic career. While collaborating on Mixr, I was also leading the Tap In prototype team, curating this portfolio, and juggling studio work in Visual Design II. I learned how to prioritize effectively, maintain deep focus, and balance parallel workstreams to give every project the rigor it deserved.
  • Every design team brings a distinct dynamic. Adapting to new collaboration styles across different teams taught me how to communicate with empathy, actively listen, and harmonize distinct perspectives into unified design choices.
  • Navigating ambiguity was a key milestone. Rather than waiting for strict direction, our team embraced autonomy, using user feedback and goal-directed milestones to pave our own roadmap to a successful final prototype.
  • Flexibility is an essential design asset. Constraints and scopes shift constantly in product development, and the ability to pivot decisively kept our momentum steady from kickoff to showcase.

Reel: A Movie and Show discussion
rival for Letterboxd

Letterboxd is the go-to movie review platform, but television and anime fans are often left underserved. Reel is a collaborative mobile prototype designed to challenge conventions in film and TV discussion. We followed Goal-Directed Design methodology, conducting in-depth user interviews, competitive analysis, and usability testing to craft an inclusive community space. This project spanned from August 2025 to November 2025, utilizing FigJam and Figma.

Interaction Design I • Aug 2025 - Nov 2025
•

The Problem:

Our focus centered on identifying and solving the friction points prevalent across incumbent review platforms.

  • Existing platforms focus almost exclusively on feature films, leaving television series and episodic storytelling neglected.
  • Letterboxd poses a steep learning curve for casual users and relies on a dated interface hierarchy.
  • As a review platform, Letterboxd offers limited real-time social engagement and community discussion features.

Solutions:

Improving the reviewing and social experience.

📺

Inclusion

A unified feed celebrating films, TV shows, and anime series alike, bridging gaps left by single-medium competitors.

✨

Clean UI

Streamlined layout engineered for immediate learnability, prioritizing direct ratings, cast notes, and episode guides.

💬

Discussions

Rich conversational threads and spoiler-shielded replies matching familiar, modern social interaction patterns.

🔍

Discoverability

Curated release schedules displaying exact premiere dates and streaming platform availability in one place.

Lessons Learned:

  • Stepped into the role of a dedicated team collaborator. Bouncing ideas off teammates and navigating conflicting creative viewpoints sharpened my active listening and design consensus skills.
  • Design tokens and variables should be established on day one. Introducing dark mode late in the lifecycle demonstrated why a solid foundation saves massive refactoring effort later on.
  • Documentation is paramount. Keeping detailed sprint backlogs ensures that high-impact features are accounted for early rather than rushed at final delivery.
  • File hygiene matters: naming frames, maintaining autolayout rules, and keeping asset libraries organized prevents compounding technical debt as deadlines approach.

Milestone Comics Rebrand

Milestone Media was founded in 1993 by Black creatives to address the exclusion of Black characters and storytellers in mainstream comic books. Static, Icon, Rocket, and Hardware are among the iconic heroes created by Milestone Media. The company later merged into the DC Universe in 2008. Over 18 years later, these legends returned to the DC continuity, and the brand called for a major revamp. For this project, I created three core deliverables: rebranding their web presence, designing an original character and comic series (SOUL), and illustrating a variant cover for the “New History of the DC Universe: The Dakota Incident” event using Figma, Photoshop, and Paint.net.

Visual Design II • March 2026 - April 2026

Milestone Media Website Rebrand

For a comic publisher’s website, the original felt heavily tied to standard newsprint. Preserving the signature white, black, and red color scheme alongside comic panel collages, I built a fresh interface evoking a true “Rebirth” feel. Heroes break through dynamic panels while the responsive layout reads with the energy and rhythm of a comic book.

Old Design
New Design

Soul: A New Milestone Series

Milestone champions Black culture, but as someone with heritage from Ghana, I felt West African cultural influences were underrepresented in the roster. That became the genesis of SOUL.

SOUL centers on Benjamin Boateng (“Benji”), a young teen whose family hails from West Africa. Following an intense emotional catalyst, Benji awakens latent spiritual abilities granting heightened perception and kinetic movement beyond standard physical limits. As his perception of the world expands, he quickly discovers that not all spirits and individuals surrounding him are as benevolent as they appear.

  • When envisioning “soul,” I think of internal authenticity and raw expression; the visual concept frames Benji’s silhouette with luminous internal energy showing who he is within.
  • Carrying over the sharp red accent color maintains strong brand synergy with the wider Milestone Universe.
  • Intricate West African cultural line art within the silhouette celebrates continental heritage, weaving identity directly into his heroic visual design.

“New History of the DC Universe: The Dakota Incident” Variant Cover

To celebrate the Milestone and DC crossover, I designed a collector-grade variant cover highlighting the core heroes uniting against new threats.
